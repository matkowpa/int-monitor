What I learned:

Intrum's credit story deteriorated today. S&P Global Ratings published a research update revising its outlook on Intrum AB, writing that the company's third-quarter results came in below the agency's previous expectations — notably in the servicing business — and that, in S&P's view, recovery in 2027 is uncertain ([S&P research update](https://spcom-disclosure-next.use2.prod.ratings.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/type/HTML/id/3655277)). S&P also states that weaker results impair Intrum's access to financial markets, increasing refinancing risks. Swedish financial press confirmed the timing this morning: DI reports that S&P lowered Intrum's outlook ([DI](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiakFVX3lxTE5PcjBQeV91M1RDVWtvTEQ3SlVZSzY5ODVjb3k0bVJYYzVibHJCX0FVY2o3WGkxdGxkME5yd1lOV0Q2dU9vQWVTdy1ibWtfandhQll2aVNqZlNrdUFnaTdDVndyWlBPUjU5SHc?oc=5)), Finanswatch frames it as a warning that Intrum's refinancing risk is being raised ([Finanswatch](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiYkFVX3lxTE0xcGlrTkdJZy1COWdId1V2X3ptQV93SnVIQ0RuWXdjVHBsNVJud3VzYUJaNkVxR3RiRDJWNDdGbWFpZ0J0VkQxbUlWVEZqcENGSWF0TVZWakhYQmRaQlhFWmd3?oc=5)), and a DI piece from yesterday evening is headlined "Sänkta kreditutsikter för Intrum: 'Speglar risken'" ([DI](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMie0FVX3lxTE1VaWR4RmFFX2FpOTRYRF9XUGhjSy1fWkQ0akxmSzRYeTJkNzhxWkF2cWlYc09WVnM3ajF6VlFoWmFIb19iMWY3X3JUNFMyNmg3OEdPRXJqTlZqVG5haExRaEFsZnRHR1RrVDNLQ0FHdWRTMy13Smw3c0xjNA?oc=5)).

The Q3 backdrop explains the move. Intrum released its Q3 2026 interim report on 23 October, with coverage highlighting a deleveraging focus and continued servicing pressure ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:efe8d339e90a6:0-intrum-ab-intrum-releases-q3-2026-interim-report/)); the company had announced the report would land at 07:00 CET with a 09:00 CET webcast presentation ([Intrum press release](https://www.intrum.com/investors/interim-report-archive/interim-report-archive-article/?id=c68d924b-bf8a-4094-929a-8bbc78d48199)). On its investor page, Intrum reports that a capital raise, asset sale and good cost control brought the Servicing leverage ratio down to 4.5x from 5.7x at year-end ([Intrum financial information](https://www.intrum.com/investors/financial-information/)). For scale, the same page shows full-year 2025 income of SEK 17,030m, adjusted EBIT of SEK 5,345m, cash EBITDA of SEK 9,098m and adjusted EPS of SEK 18.

The outlook cut marks a reversal from earlier in the year, when S&P upgraded Intrum to 'B-' after shareholders approved a fully guaranteed SEK 7.5 billion equity capital raise — a transaction the agency then said materially reduced risks to the group's financial sustainability ([S&P upgrade to 'B-'](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/type/HTML/id/3582408)). The new research update indicates the Q3 servicing miss has eroded that comfort.

Separately today, PwC says it advised Intrum on the sale of Intrum Baltics ([PwC via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMivAFBVV95cUxNZExjWlhPYlIwZ2l3UU1jV21ZQkdxbzJYNVhzVWljSWZzdGJHbFp0WFBBOEYzX3QwQmk1UThMYTBBTmJKYXQ5SEY0RzFBN2ZVNVVzcTNwekdxZzlEbW1xVHNOeG5BVURxbXlyOGU2bXU5N002T0dmaFl2bjI4ZVBUZzdtSWFmWTRtS2pOYkJPbEQzSG5DNkRtZy1ZdjhTRmZyWUpYQ2xKSXQ1ejRnZGNWN3dRc0g4eVFZMnJmag?oc=5)) — evidence that portfolio pruning continues, though the item discloses no buyer or proceeds, and I can't confirm from these sources whether this is the "asset sale" credited in the leverage improvement. A minor insider signal: Intrum's HR chief increased her shareholding ([MarketScreener](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMingFBVV95cUxPUkhaVXpiV1d2YW1TOWdxOEQwMXBfWXVhZHlfa2RxLUZ3UndIVzEtVEhqMzRaYXA0TXhibjhiZ3dBdzIySVNFWTV4cm9KNGRpZkQydERvYklRV2ctbmJQRkx4ZEE4aXRraW1hVzJnRWpkQTliMExDY1FFRmpkbTVaN2tJZU9nNVdvdlUwa3BLeWxoS2hQWlZfNHNfX3RqUQ?oc=5)).

To keep evidence and interpretation apart: the below-expectation Q3 results, uncertain 2027 recovery and elevated refinancing risk are S&P's assessments, not company guidance; the 4.5x leverage figure is Intrum's own reporting; and the Baltics sale's financial terms remain unknown from today's coverage.
