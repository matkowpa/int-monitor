What I learned: Intrum's most recent datapoint is its third-quarter report published on 23 October, which the company itself summarizes as "Balance sheet strengthened, Servicing income still under pressure and Investing volumes increased" ([Intrum press page](https://www.intrum.com/press/)). Coverage of the Q3 earnings call ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9139822/intrum-ab-itjty-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-leverage-cut-to-45-germany-goodwill-written-off-and-cost-cuts-accelerate-toward-sek11-billion)) flags three headline items: leverage cut to 4.5, a goodwill write-off in Germany, and cost cuts accelerating toward SEK 11 billion. Read together, the release describes balance-sheet progress continuing even as servicing revenue stays weak; the German goodwill impairment, as reported in the call highlights, signals that expectations for that business have been marked down rather than improved.

The deleveraging theme runs through all of 2026. In late January Intrum presented "Intrum 2030," the result of a strategic review with new financial targets, focused short-term on deleveraging and derisking ([financial reports page](https://www.intrum.com/investors/financial-reports-presentations/)). That was underpinned on 7 May by a fully guaranteed SEK 7.5 billion equity capital raise "to accelerate its strategy and deleveraging path" ([press release](https://www.intrum.com/press/press-releases/press-release-article/?id=7b9715a7-0214-404f-8567-bce3baac39c8)). The Q1 interim report noted the servicing leverage ratio was largely unchanged quarter-over-quarter, supported by consolidation of the Savoy Group JV ([Q1 report](https://storage.mfn.se/b4740a02-a6d3-47aa-b86d-deb28834665f/iab-q1-report-2026-eng.pdf)) — so the leverage reduction to 4.5 by Q3 suggests the equity raise and subsequent execution have since moved the needle. The Q2 report (28 August) described a successful capital raise strengthening the financial position ([Nasdaq release](https://view.news.eu.nasdaq.com/view?id=ba1e76f303f20878ca95226ebb76826f5&lang=en&src=micro)), with costs developing according to plan, but servicing income growth in Traditional markets not sufficient to offset declines in Specialised markets ([Q2 report](https://storage.mfn.se/94ec9243-ed46-4a6f-8c67-3b20873b582a/interim-report-second-quarter-2026-intrum.pdf)) — a revenue-mix pressure the Q3 title confirms is still in place.

Mid-year governance and credit events frame the trajectory: an Extraordinary General Meeting on 9 June passed resolutions and the board moved the Q2 publication date ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/modular_finance:85ba8377781ac:0-announcement-from-intrum-s-extraordinary-general-meeting/)), and on 17 June S&P upgraded Intrum's issuer credit rating to B- with positive outlook ([press release](https://storage.mfn.se/2b072e5f-6f0f-45c1-825f-4e89ba702a3e/intrum-credit-rating-upgraded-by-sandp-global-ratings-with-positive-outlook.pdf)). That upgrade coming after the capital raise suggests agencies viewed the recapitalization as materially de-risking the balance sheet; the positive outlook implies further upgrade potential if deleveraging continues, though that is inference, not stated criteria. On funding, the maturity profile page was updated as of 14 September and reiterates a diversified funding base ([maturity profile](https://www.intrum.com/investors/debt-credit-rating/maturity-profile/)), while the credit-rating page positions external ratings as necessary to a "robust financial platform" ([credit rating](https://www.intrum.com/investors/debt-credit-rating/credit-rating/)). No new RSS items have appeared since the last run, so the 23 October Q3 release remains the freshest verified datapoint; anything after that date is not captured in these results.
