What I learned: Intrum's governance cycle is the day's main story. This morning the company moved to assemble its nomination committee ahead of the 2027 annual general meeting: per [NPL Confidential](https://nplconfidential.com/nomination-committee-in-intrum-2/), the Chairman of the Board has been assigned to convene a Nomination Committee for the AGM 2027, to consist of shareholder representatives. No company confirmation appears in today's results, so this should be treated as unconfirmed reporting; if accurate, a Spanish divestment would be a significant portfolio move for what Intrum itself bills as Europe's leading provider of credit management services.

For context, the most recent company release in the results, published 28.08.2026, describes a successful capital raise in the second quarter that strengthened Intrum's financial position, alongside continued execution of its Intrum 2030 strategy ([Intrum press release](https://www.intrum.com/press/press-releases/press-release-article/?id=db1e69a4-78c7-4f17-8049-6ea82a268667)). That backdrop — a recently shored-up balance sheet and a multi-year strategy in progress — frames both today's governance churn and the reported Spanish divestment interest as part of a broader reshaping phase. Watch items: the named composition of the new nomination committee, and whether the Spain report gets confirmed or denied.
