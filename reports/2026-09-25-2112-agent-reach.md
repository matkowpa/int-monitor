What I learned: The freshest development is from 18 September, when Intrum announced an agreement to co-invest in and become servicer to a Nordic non-performing loan portfolio ([Inderes](https://www.inderes.se/en/releases/intrum-to-co-invest-and-become-servicer-to-nordic-npl-portfolio)). The structure matters: pairing a servicing mandate with a co-investment stake is an asset-light way to generate fee income and upside without fully loading the balance sheet — the kind of transaction a post-restructuring Intrum would want to signal.

Liability management also ran into September: on 8 September Intrum announced the results of tender offers for outstanding senior secured exchange notes of Intrum Investments and Financing AB ([Inderes](https://www.inderes.dk/en/releases/intrum-announces-the-results-of-the-tender-offers-for-outstanding-senior-secured-exchange-notes-of-intrum-investments-and-financing-ab-3)). The available snippet does not disclose take-up figures, but the exercise reads as a continuation of the balance-sheet clean-up that defined the summer.

That clean-up was the Q2 story, reported on 28 August under the headline "strengthens financial position in second quarter with successful capital raise" ([Inderes](https://www.inderes.se/en/releases/intrum-strengthens-financial-position-in-second-quarter-with-successful-capital-raise)). Per the interim report, key balance-sheet strengthening initiatives completed in July — a SEK 7.5bn fully guaranteed capital raise and a SEK 2.4bn portfolio sale — contributed to rating upgrades by S&P and Moody's to B- and B3 respectively, while the operational transformation continued with costs developing according to plan ([interim report PDF](https://storage.mfn.se/94ec9243-ed46-4a6f-8c67-3b20873b582a/interim-report-second-quarter-2026-intrum.pdf)).

The ratings trajectory was set earlier: S&P's 17 June research update upgraded Intrum to 'B-', with the title pointing to capital as the driver ([S&P Global Ratings](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/type/HTML/id/3582408)). That carries extra weight given Intrum's own investor materials describe an external rating as a necessity for maintaining a robust financial platform ([Intrum credit rating page](https://www.intrum.com/investors/debt-credit-rating/credit-rating/)) — the upgrades are plausibly more than symbolic for funding access, though that last step is interpretation.

The capital raise itself was announced on 7 May alongside Q1 results, described as intended to accelerate deleveraging ([Intrum press release](https://www.intrum.com/press/press-releases/press-release-article/?id=7b9715a7-0214-404f-8567-bce3baac39c8)). The Q1 report — the first quarter of new strategy implementation — showed overall development according to plan, with cost reductions ahead of plan but servicing income slightly behind, both compounded by FX; the servicing leverage ratio was largely unchanged quarter-on-quarter, supported by the consolidation of a joint venture ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/intrum-iab-q1-report-2026-eng-ce7f58d3d988fe27)).

The strategic frame for all of this is "Intrum 2030," the direction and new financial targets presented on 29 January following a strategic review ([Intrum IR](https://www.intrum.com/investors/financial-reports-presentations/)). Read together, the sequence — capital raise, portfolio sale, tender offers, ratings upgrades, and now a Nordic NPL co-investment and servicing mandate — traces a company moving from balance-sheet repair toward redeployment into its core servicing business. That synthesis is interpretation, but the direction of travel across the sources is consistent. No new RSS items since the previous run.
