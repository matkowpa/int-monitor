What I learned: Intrum's most recent disclosed move is a co-investment in a Nordic non-performing loan (NPL) portfolio, with Swedbank as the seller. A [Finwire report carried by MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/intrum-co-invests-in-nordic-npl-portfolio-swedbank-the-seller-ce785adadb80ff23) dated September 18, 2026, states that Intrum and Brocc Finance are jointly acquiring the portfolio. The snippet is truncated, so the portfolio's size, price, and Intrum's share of the co-investment are not verifiable from this source. The deal reads as a continuation of Intrum's core debt-purchase activity in its home Nordic market, though the report itself only describes the transaction rather than any strategic rationale.

On the credit side, the trajectory has been improving since mid-June. [Intrum announced on June 12 that Moody's had upgraded its credit rating](https://www.inderes.fi/en/releases/intrum-credit-rating-upgraded-by-moodys-following-shareholder-approval-of-capital-raise), with the upgrade explicitly tied to shareholder approval of a capital raise. Days later, [a June 17 announcement reported that S&P Global Ratings upgraded Intrum's issuer credit rating to B-, with a positive outlook](https://www.inderes.fi/en/releases/intrum-credit-rating-upgraded-by-sandp-global-ratings-with-positive-outlook). Taken together — and this is interpretation rather than stated fact — the two upgrades suggest both major agencies viewed the shareholder-approved recapitalization as strengthening Intrum's balance sheet, and S&P's positive outlook points to the possibility of further improvement.

Two caveats: both rating actions date from June and predate the September portfolio purchase, so no source connects the two events; and no new RSS items have arrived since the previous run, leaving the MarketScreener report as the freshest available information.
