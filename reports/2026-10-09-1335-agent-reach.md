The headline gives no buyer or seller, so the safe read is that ownership is churning around a sub-1% block — worth watching for follow-on disclosures rather than drawing conclusions from a single print.

The trade lands against the backdrop of a major balance-sheet reset. Intrum's investor relations page describes a fully guaranteed capital raise of SEK 7.5bn, explicitly framed as a way to accelerate the deleveraging path, reduce financial risk, and give the company the strength and flexibility to deliver on its "Intrum 2030" strategy announced on 29 January ([Intrum IR](https://www.intrum.com/investors/)). A Milbank LLP client note dated 30 July 2026 confirms the full scope of the package: the firm advised Intrum on the SEK 7.5bn capital raise together with a €525m senior secured notes issuance, an exchange offer, and consent solicitations — which Milbank characterizes as a series of landmark transactions ([Milbank](https://www.milbank.com/en/news/milbank-advises-intrum-on-its-sek-75b-capital-raise-and-euro525m-senior-secured-notes-issuance-exchange-offer-and-consent-solicitations.html)). Read together, the release and the advisor note signal a comprehensive liability-management exercise rather than a simple equity top-up, which would explain why creditor-side consent solicitations were needed.

On the operating side, Intrum announced on 18 September that it will co-invest alongside Brocc Finance to acquire a non-performing consumer finance loan portfolio in the Nordics, with Intrum also taking on the servicing role ([Placera](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/intrum-intrum-to-co-invest-and-become-servicer-to-nordic-npl-portfolio-20260918)). That structure — co-investment plus servicer mandate — looks consistent with the post-raise playbook: deploying capital into portfolios while earning fee income, rather than carrying the full exposure on balance sheet.
