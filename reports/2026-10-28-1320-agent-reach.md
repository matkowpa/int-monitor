The longer arc behind today's tension is visible in the semantic search results. In June, S&P upgraded Intrum to 'B-' and moved it off CreditWatch Positive with a positive outlook, citing shareholder approval of a fully guaranteed SEK capital raise and solid deleveraging ([S&P Global Ratings](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/sourceId/101691797)). The Q2 2026 presentation in late August showed the same split personality: the SEK 7.5 billion capital raise was completed and balance-sheet metrics improved, but the company faced mounting pressure on servicing income and revenue headwinds ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/intrum-q2-2026-slides-balance-sheet-gains-offset-by-revenue-headwinds-93CH-4880676)).

My read: the balance-sheet repair story the rating agencies endorsed appears intact, but the persistent top-line weakness flagged in Q2 is what's now driving estimate cuts, a slashed price target and rising short interest into the autumn. The CEO's modest purchase reads as a contrarian signal, not a turning point. Caveat: today's RSS items are headline-level only, so specifics behind the short-selling acceleration and the analyst revisions deserve verification before acting on them.
