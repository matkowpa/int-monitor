What I learned: Intrum's most recent disclosed move is an agreement announced on 18 September to co-invest in a Nordic non-performing-loan (NPL) portfolio while also taking on the servicing mandate for it ([Inderes](https://www.inderes.se/en/releases/intrum-to-co-invest-and-become-servicer-to-nordic-npl-portfolio)). The release describes Intrum, which it calls Europe's leading credit management services company, entering the arrangement as both co-investor and servicer; read against its servicing-led model, this signals the company is using its platform to win portfolio mandates while rebuilding its own balance sheet.

The financial backdrop is a mixed Q2 2026. On 28 August, Intrum reported falling Q2 income and flagged a tougher servicing outlook, saying weaker-than-expected developments in some markets would make it more challenging to keep servicing income broadly flat this year, with portfolio deterioration and slower sales weighing on the quarter ([Investing.com](https://in.investing.com/news/earnings/intrum-flags-tougher-2026-servicing-outlook-as-q2-income-falls-5575249), [Investing.com CA](https://ca.investing.com/news/earnings/intrum-flags-tougher-2026-servicing-outlook-as-q2-income-falls-4820872)). The same day, the company framed the quarter around a successful capital raise and a strengthened financial position ([Inderes](https://www.inderes.se/en/releases/intrum-strengthens-financial-position-in-second-quarter-with-successful-capital-raise)).

That balance-sheet repair is the clearest positive thread. According to the Q2 interim report, key balance-sheet strengthening initiatives completed in July — a SEK 7.5bn fully guaranteed capital raise and a SEK 2.4bn portfolio sale — contributed to rating upgrades by S&P and Moody's to B- and B3, while the operational transformation continues with costs developing according to plan ([Interim report Q2 2026, PDF](https://storage.mfn.se/94ec9243-ed46-4a6f-8c67-3b20873b582a/interim-report-second-quarter-2026-intrum.pdf)). The capital raise was first announced on 7 May alongside Q1 results, with the stated aim of accelerating deleveraging ([Intrum press release](https://www.intrum.com/press/press-releases/press-release-article/?id=7b9715a7-0214-404f-8567-bce3baac39c8)). Intrum's investor pages show the debt maturity profile was updated as of 24 July 2026 and maintain overviews of the current S&P and Moody's ratings ([maturity profile](https://www.intrum.com/investors/debt-credit-rating/maturity-profile/), [credit rating](https://www.intrum.com/investors/debt-credit-rating/credit-rating/)).

Strategically, these moves sit under "Intrum 2030," the strategic review presented on 29 January that set the company's direction and new financial targets, with a defined short-term strategy focus ([Intrum financial reports & presentations](https://www.intrum.com/investors/financial-reports-presentations/)). My read: the September NPL co-investment suggests Intrum is leveraging its upgraded ratings and repaired capital base to return to portfolio investment — historically capital-intensive — even as the Q2 commentary shows the core servicing business faces 2026 headwinds.
