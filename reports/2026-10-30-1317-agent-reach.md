What I learned: Intrum's Q3 2026 report, published on 23 October, shows the company making real progress on deleveraging. The [Swedish interim report](https://storage.mfn.se/db90f9f2-f363-4fa9-a984-ac4524d4a3a9/intrum-delarsrapport-tredje-kvartalet-2026.pdf) credits capital raising, asset divestments and good cost control for improving the Servicing leverage ratio to 4.5x, down from 5.7x at the end of 2025 — a figure [Investing.com's coverage of the results slides](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/intrum-q3-2026-slides-leverage-improves-as-germany-writeoff-hits-93CH-4927511) confirms. The same report notes that the investment pace increased while returns remained high, with 617 MSEK invested during the quarter. The improvement is not purely clean, though: Investing.com frames the quarter as deleveraging progress paired with operational challenges in key markets, highlighting a write-off in Germany that weighed on results. The [presentation press release](https://storage.mfn.se/4577af3b-b709-437d-8b04-e50782eb0cfa/press-release-presentation-of-intrums-results-for-the-third-quarter-2026.pdf) confirms results were released at 07:00 CET on 23 October and presented via webcast.

Today's headlines point to active liability management on top of that deleveraging story. Per [EFN via Google News](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMihgFBVV95cUxNMzFZaE12cFlMVE5RNzZVZl9BdDZ4NTZ6S0dROEx6Q1RGZWZNXzREdnNPX09VZ1l4SDdXb0NiTFFJc0VoVm9LMERlQUJmeFZHQy1zSjR1MjZVWXJERkFneGY2V01iNHRTb2o0VC1fQVdLdDlGTVU3NGpMbHpMUVJpcTBBak1LZw?oc=5), Intrum wants to repurchase bonds for up to €100 million. That sits naturally alongside the company's stated financing policy — its [maturity profile page](https://www.intrum.com/investors/debt-credit-rating/maturity-profile/), updated 14 September 2026, describes Treasury's aim of securing a diversified funding base — suggesting a deliberate effort to shape the debt stack as leverage falls.

The other thread is ownership: a Norwegian investor has emerged as a major shareholder and is increasing its position. [EFN](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiWkFVX3lxTE45YmFyQXpGeUk1ZXMzVV9jTzlsaEJaQi1WMS04TjNuZm9tbXJ2SFF0YkZPaEZPRlpfMTUxVV9XN3J6VTFCX3JXWHZtbTBScWRMVHVoM3hCQmdfQQ?oc=5) reports the Norwegian holder is adding to its stake, and [DI](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMieEFVX3lxTFAwVGJob2s4MGJCUjlxbnNHVDV0d2s2aGNKYkVXN3h0dGtJdzlObTYzWXNzeGg2a1gzMTVCQTV4dVo1SnJ0X09iTExWa1B4X1prZWlyOXBNWDItV1RCbG10dVBsTGcyV1Foc0tsWV9iM0U2cGhnaEZENg?oc=5) likewise reports the major shareholder increasing exposure. A follow-up [EFN piece](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMieEFVX3lxTFBhQW82ZWF4TkNBWWJvOU9qVm9QZVBWYU9pWEJfT2RfUS1YcktiWkM3Zl9kbjBVU1FoU09KOHAtekJ2bWhsN0EwWUhnZl8tV2MtZ1d2MGhwaDRsWlROamdXNlNBZjVLTjlQYWtsejhqcTlIa0ZZaFBuWA?oc=5) quotes the new Norwegian holder describing the position as "a financial investment" — which signals conviction in the equity story rather than strategic intent, though these are headline-level reports and the details are not yet verified.

Net read: a quarter of measurable balance-sheet repair, followed within a week by a bond buyback proposal and visible accumulation by a new large shareholder — a combination that suggests the market is starting to price Intrum as a recovering credit and equity rather than a distressed one.
